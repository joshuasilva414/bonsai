# Personalized learning: the model behind an adaptive curriculum

Research date: 2026-08-10

## Bottom line

A tree-shaped **scope and sequence** is a useful author and learner orientation: it makes the course legible and provides a default order. It is not, by itself, a personalized-learning model. A personalized system needs an explicit model of outcomes and dependencies, a continually updated **learner profile**, and a short-horizon **personal learning plan** that is revised from formative evidence. The tree should therefore remain one projection of a richer learning graph, not the mechanism that decides learning.

This is a design inference from the sources below, rather than a claim that a particular database model improves learning. The key pedagogical distinction is between an authored structure and an iterative instructional decision cycle.

## What educators actually personalize

1. **Goals and evidence, not simply content order.** Competency-based education (CBE) organizes progress around what a learner can demonstrate, rather than time spent; it permits flexible pacing, paths, and supports. IES describes personalized learning similarly: it responds to learner needs and interests while allowing flexibility in pace and pathway to mastery. [IES on CBE](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/resource/fact-sheetinfographicfaq/competency-based-education-practice), [IES on personalized-learning implementation](https://ies.ed.gov/learn/blog/understanding-personalized-learning-implementation-data).
2. **A recurring formative-assessment cycle.** Educators identify a target, teach and elicit evidence, interpret it, give targeted feedback or support, then adjust instruction. This is not a final quiz plus a status label; it is ongoing diagnosis during learning. [IES/WWC formative-assessment cycle](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Docs/PracticeGuide/508_WWCPG_SecondaryWriting_122719.pdf), [IES review of formative assessment](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/resource-library/report/descriptive-study/formative-assessment-and-elementary-school-student-academic-achievement-review-evidence).
3. **Learning conditions as well as sequence.** Strong practice includes spaced review, retrieval practice, worked examples alternating with problem solving, multiple representations, and deep explanatory questions. A planner must schedule these instructional moves, not just choose the next node. [IES/WWC practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1).
4. **Agency with guardrails.** The learner takes part in setting goals, seeing progress, seeking feedback, and reflecting. ISTE calls this the *Empowered Learner*; the NCES personalized-learning data guide describes a learner profile and plan co-developed with the learner. [ISTE Standards for Students](https://iste.org/standards/students), [NCES Forum Guide to Personalized Learning Data](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED599382.pdf).
5. **Multiple legitimate means to a goal.** Universal Design for Learning (UDL) separates a learning goal from a single fixed means of reaching or demonstrating it: offer options for engagement, representation, action, and expression while retaining the same rigorous target. [CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/).

Personalized does not mean isolated or entirely machine-directed. The NCES guide explicitly includes collaboration, teacher guidance, mentoring, and projects; its learner profile and plan are co-developed, not silently imposed by a system. [NCES Forum Guide to Personalized Learning Data](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED599382.pdf).

Adaptive tutoring has evidence of benefit, but it is not magic. A meta-analysis of 50 controlled evaluations reported a median improvement of 0.66 standard deviations over conventional instruction; effects depended substantially on alignment between instructional and assessment objectives. That supports careful outcome/evidence alignment, not a claim that any adaptive route will work. [Kulik & Fletcher (2016)](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315581420).

## Is a tree-style learning path enough?

No, unless the product only promises a clear, default course outline. A strict forward tree fails to represent several normal instructional moves:

- an objective may require several prerequisites and support later objectives in more than one branch;
- evidence can trigger remediation, a prerequisite bridge, extra practice, an alternative representation, or delayed review;
- a learner may advance in one strand while revisiting another (interleaving and spacing);
- the learner can pursue a goal-driven detour without changing the canonical curriculum.

Use the tree for **containment** and a human-readable outline. Add typed links for **prerequisite**, **supports/depends on**, **related/transfer**, and **review**; let the learner plan be an ordered, revisable sequence through that graph. A roadmap should show the next few planned learning experiences and why they were chosen, while the outline remains available for orientation and agency.

The closest established term is **learning progression** (also *learning trajectory*, *construct map*, or *construct model*). The National Academies characterizes it as increasingly sophisticated thinking over time, including intermediate ideas and misconceptions, used to guide both instruction and assessment. It is a hypothesized, evidence-informed route—not an inevitable single route for every learner. [National Academies: *How People Learn II*](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/9), [National Academies: *Science Teachers' Learning*](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/21836/chapter/4).

## Vocabulary to add to Bonsai

| Product term | Standard educational language | Meaning for Bonsai |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Course tree | **Scope and sequence** / curriculum map | Canonical hierarchy and default authored order. |
| A teachable leaf | **Learning objective** or **competency** | An observable target; a competency should have a performance criterion, not only a title. |
| Planned next steps | **Personal learning plan/pathway** | A short, revisable route with learner goal, rationale, pace, and deferred work. |
| Current learner state | **Learner profile** / learner model | Strengths, needs, goals, interests, access needs, prior evidence, misconceptions, and supports; it is editable and evolves. |
| Node progression | **Learning progression/trajectory** | A domain hypothesis about how understanding can grow, including likely intermediate conceptions. |
| Evidence that changes the plan | **Formative assessment** | Low-stakes evidence used during instruction to choose feedback, support, or the next learning experience. |
| “Mastered” | **Demonstrated mastery/proficiency** | A claim tied to an explicit criterion and evidence; reading or a self-report alone is not mastery. |
| Agent’s next-step logic | **Adaptive sequencing** / pedagogical model | Chooses content *and* instructional move: advance, scaffold, retrieve, practice, interleave, revisit, or change representation. |
| Help before independence | **Scaffolding** | Temporary prompts, examples, hints, or reduced complexity that fade as performance improves. |
| Inclusive alternatives | **Universal Design for Learning (UDL)** | Multiple means of engagement, representation, and action/expression, while preserving the outcome. |
| Learner choice and reflection | **Learner agency** / **self-regulated learning** | The learner sets or negotiates goals, monitors progress, uses feedback, and reflects. |

Avoid making a fixed `visual/auditory/kinesthetic learner` label part of the learner profile. The influential review found insufficient evidence for matching instruction to such labels; provide useful multiple representations and choose supports from observed task performance instead. [Pashler et al. (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x).

## Practical product model

Keep four distinct things rather than collapsing them into `tree + node status`:

1. **Competency map:** objectives, performance criteria, canonical containment, and dependency/transfer edges.
2. **Learning progression:** authored or evidence-informed descriptions of intermediate understanding, common misconceptions, and suitable instructional strategies for an objective.
3. **Learner profile and evidence ledger:** direct assessment evidence, confidence/self-report, attempts, annotations, accessibility preferences, goals, and interests—each with provenance and uncertainty.
4. **Personal learning plan:** an explainable, short-horizon route of learning experiences. Each step names its objective, instructional move, reason, success evidence, and review schedule. The plan can be replaced ahead of the current step without rewriting the canonical curriculum or history.

For the agent’s navigation view, make the personal learning plan primary, with a one- or two-hop local graph neighborhood and an expandable scope-and-sequence outline. This gives it the temporal context to plan, the dependency context to remediate, and the human-readable context to orient the learner—without asking the model to infer a plan from an entire static tree every turn.

## Sources and limits

The sources are official education frameworks/research syntheses and peer-reviewed meta-analysis; they support the instructional model and terminology, not a particular graph database choice or an autonomous-agent policy. Learning science is domain-, learner-, and implementation-sensitive, so Bonsai should record decisions and test outcomes rather than treat any route policy as settled.
